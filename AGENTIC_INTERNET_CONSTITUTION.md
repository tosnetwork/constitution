## 0. Status, Authority, and Interpretation

This document states design principles for an open Agentic Internet and the
highest-level governance principles by which the TOS ecosystem should be
designed and judged. It is called a Constitution because it must constrain
founders, maintainers, validators, operators, applications, and autonomous
Agents alike. It may be called the TOS Bible informally, but it is not sacred,
infallible, or beyond challenge. A living technical society requires reasoned
amendment rather than unquestioned doctrine.

The Agentic Internet is not a product, repository, chain, company, model, or
marketplace. It is an open network of humans, autonomous Agents, services,
capabilities, communication systems, and settlement domains that can discover
one another and form verifiable, bounded relationships through interoperable
protocols.

TOS proposes this constitutional design for adoption within its own ecosystem.
Adoption does not give TOS authority over independent projects or over the
Internet. Third parties may adopt these principles, implement compatible
protocols in independent repositories, and operate independent infrastructure
without adopting TOS governance. Conformance to an open protocol creates no
political, organizational, or fiduciary subordination to TOS.

This Constitution becomes governing only after an explicit public
ratification record identifies the adopting authority, repository commit,
date, and scope. Its presence on a branch, in a draft pull request, or in a
working tree does not itself constitute ratification.

After ratification:

- this Constitution governs the interpretation of TOS ecosystem direction,
  institutional boundaries, and change proposals within its adopted scope;
- independent adopters are governed only by the scope and version they
  explicitly adopt, and TOS ratification does not bind them;
- exact protocol bytes, signatures, schemas, state transitions, and consensus
  behavior remain governed by their adopted normative specifications and
  deployed code;
- current finalized chain state remains operationally authoritative and cannot
  be rewritten by this document;
- private Agreements, applicable law, and third-party rights are not displaced
  merely by a repository document; and
- aspirational language does not prove implementation, deployment, adoption,
  safety, legal compliance, or production readiness.

This Constitution is an institutional and technical governance document, not
legal code or legal advice. Each Constitutional Adopter remains responsible for
identifying applicable jurisdictions, legally accountable entities, mandatory
rights and duties, and conflicts that cannot be resolved by protocol rules.

The key words **MUST**, **MUST NOT**, **REQUIRED**, **SHOULD**, **SHOULD NOT**,
and **MAY** express constitutional strength. Exact protocol specifications
should continue to define their own normative-language convention.

## 1. Preamble

Knowledge is distributed. Human purposes differ. Agents are bounded. Markets
discover information but do not automatically create justice, trust, or
safety. Organizations coordinate efficiently in some conditions and become
dangerous concentrations of power in others. Software can enforce commitments,
but code can also preserve bugs, privilege, and accidental harm.

An Agentic Internet therefore exists neither as an omniscient central machine
nor as an ungoverned market. It provides common, verifiable protocols through
which humans, Agents, organizations, and services may discover one another,
make bounded commitments, cooperate, exchange value, prove outcomes, resolve
conflicts, and leave relationships they no longer accept. TOS exists to help
design, implement, and operate one open part of that larger Internet.

The constitutional objective is:

> **Local judgment, protocol coordination; minimum authority, verifiable
> commitments; polycentric governance, meaningful exit; bounded experiments,
> no automatic expansion of power.**

The TOS project owner begins as the founding constitutional and institutional
designer of the TOS adoption. That role is stewardship, not ownership of the
Agentic Internet or of every participant's choices. The steward designs
conditions under which others can act safely and autonomously. The steward
does not centrally schedule every Agent, define every legitimate business,
control independent implementations, or convert private preference into
silent protocol law.

## 2. Agentic Internet and Constitutional Purpose

This Constitution has six purposes:

1. define the social and institutional principles of an open Agentic Internet;
2. apply those principles to TOS as a founding ecosystem and reference
   implementation;
3. allocate decisions to the lowest competent and accountable layer;
4. identify the facts that require global consensus and those that do not;
5. constrain authority, adaptation, emergency action, and institutional
   change; and
6. provide a repeatable test for whether a proposal belongs in a blockchain,
   a protocol, a service, an Agent, a local owner policy, or nowhere at all.

The Agentic Internet is a protocol commons, not a sovereign jurisdiction. TOS
is a voluntary technical ecosystem and the proposed founding adopter of this
Constitution. This Constitution does not claim political sovereignty over
people, territories, or external institutions. It governs TOS project conduct
and any technical or organizational surface that explicitly adopts a stated
version and scope.

Independent implementation is a design objective, not an exception. A party
may implement an adopted protocol in a different language, architecture,
repository, organization, governance model, business model, or execution
environment. Interoperability depends on observable conformance to the
normative protocol, not on reuse of TOS source code or membership in a TOS
organization.

### 2.1 Constitutional terms

| Term | Constitutional meaning |
|---|---|
| **Agentic Internet** | An open internetwork of humans, Agents, services, capabilities, transports, and authority domains that interoperate through public protocols without requiring one owner or implementation. |
| **TOS** | The broader TOS project, repository, network, product, and institutional ecosystem, whether or not every surface is included in a constitutional adoption. |
| **TOS Adoption Scope** | The exact repositories, networks, services, institutions, governance processes, and versions made subject to the Constitution by a TOS ratification record and its append-only scope register. |
| **Constitutional Adopter** | An organization, network, or community that explicitly ratifies an exact version of this Constitution for a declared scope. |
| **Protocol Implementer** | A party that implements a protocol. Implementation or protocol conformance alone does not make that party a Constitutional Adopter. |
| **Participant** | A human, organization, Agent, service, operator, or infrastructure provider acting within an adopted protocol or institution. |
| **Affected Party** | A person or organization materially affected by an action even if it is not a protocol participant or customer. |
| **Agent** | A software-controlled actor that interprets context or pursues delegated objectives. Its protocol identity and capabilities do not create unlimited authority or presumed legal personhood. |
| **Owner** | The principal that holds the adopted authority to establish, constrain, recover, transfer where permitted, or terminate an Agent or delegated relationship. Ownership does not grant authority over another party's rights or assets. |
| **Controller** | A principal or credential authorized to exercise a bounded subset of an Owner's authority. A Controller is not silently the Owner and cannot expand its own delegation. |
| **Accountable Authority** | The human, organization, governance body, or adopted rule that legitimately delegates or assumes responsibility for a consequential action. |
| **Intent** | A revocable expression of desired outcome or opportunity. An Intent is not, by itself, an accepted obligation or payment authority. |
| **Agreement** | An exact, mutually accepted commitment that identifies the applicable parties, revision, terms, authority, and evidence or settlement rules. |
| **Authority Domain** | A network, ledger, organization, policy, or other bounded system that is authoritative for a declared class of facts. |
| **Normative Specification** | The versioned text, schemas, state machines, and vectors that define required protocol behavior. |
| **Reference Implementation** | One implementation offered as evidence or tooling. It is not silently normative and receives no privileged conformance status. |
| **Protocol Conformance** | Evidence that an implementation satisfies a declared protocol version and profile. It is distinct from constitutional adoption, security certification, and production readiness. |
| **Consequential Action** | An action that can materially affect funds, rights, identity, privacy, safety, access, obligations, external systems, or irreversible state. |
| **Major Surface** | A repository, protocol, service, release, institution, or authority boundary whose failure or change could materially affect constitutional rights, shared interoperability, custody, consensus, evidence integrity, or a substantial affected population. |
| **Critical Violation** | A demonstrated or credibly imminent violation that can cause unauthorized or irreversible loss of funds, rights, identity, privacy, safety, finality, evidence integrity, or independent implementation. |
| **Verified Fact** | A claim established by the authority and verification method required for that claim. A derived view, model output, or local database record is not automatically a Verified Fact. |

Normative terms apply only to the actors and surfaces within an explicit
adoption scope. A party cannot be bound merely by receiving a message,
implementing an open protocol, appearing in an index, or being described by
another participant.

The ratification record MUST establish an append-only TOS Adoption Scope
register with accountable owners for every included Major Surface. Adding a
surface requires an explicit scope decision. Removing or renaming a surface
MUST NOT erase accrued obligations, incidents, evidence, migration duties, or
the rights of affected parties.

### 2.2 Authority and conflict rules

Different authorities answer different questions. They MUST NOT be collapsed
into one ambiguous hierarchy.

| Question | Governing authority |
|---|---|
| What institutional direction and limits has an adopter accepted? | the exact ratified Constitution and amendment record |
| What does a protocol version require? | its adopted normative specification, schemas, and vectors |
| What does an implementation actually do? | its exact source revision, build, configuration, dependencies, and observed execution |
| What canonical TOS state exists? | independently verified finalized state under the applicable TOS consensus rules |
| What did parties accept? | the exact valid Agreement and applicable external law |
| What does an off-chain signature establish? | only the assertion, purpose, scope, domain, and authority validly bound by that signature |
| What does an Agent, Gateway, indexer, marketplace, or model report? | a local assertion or projection unless separately verified by the required authority |

If these sources conflict, the system MUST preserve evidence, identify the
question and controlling authority, stop unsupported claims or unsafe new
effects, and use the applicable correction, dispute, migration, upgrade, or
amendment process. A constitutional interpretation MUST NOT rewrite finalized
state, silently change a protocol version, or retroactively alter an accepted
Agreement.

### 2.3 Canonical text and translations

The canonical constitutional text is the exact repository commit and document
digest identified in its ratification record. Mutable web pages, rendered
copies, summaries, model explanations, and chat messages are not substitutes
for that text.

The English text is authoritative for the TOS Founding Edition unless the
ratification record explicitly co-ratifies another language. Translations MUST
identify the source version and SHOULD be treated as informative when meaning
diverges. Interpretations, corrections, and amendments MUST be append-only,
versioned, and linked from the canonical record.

## 3. Foundational Social Theory

### 3.1 Polycentric governance is the governing core

This Constitution adopts Elinor Ostrom's polycentric-governance perspective as
its primary institutional foundation. Complex systems can be governed by
multiple, partially autonomous centers operating at different scales, provided that
boundaries, rule-making, monitoring, accountability, sanctions, conflict
resolution, self-organization, and nested coordination are credible.

For the Agentic Internet and TOS, this means:

- no single indexer, marketplace, model, Gateway, Carrier, wallet, oracle,
  Skill registry, MCP catalog, or project team should become universal
  commercial authority merely because it is convenient;
- local owners and Agents should retain decisions that depend on their
  circumstances, capabilities, values, and risk tolerance;
- shared infrastructure should expose verifiable facts and common rules rather
  than attempt to understand every industry; and
- governance should be nested: local policy inside organizations, interoperable
  standards across organizations, and consensus only for facts that truly
  require one common history.

### 3.2 Dispersed knowledge limits central planning

Friedrich Hayek's account of dispersed knowledge supplies the epistemic rule.
Knowledge of time, place, capability, cost, trust, and opportunity is never
fully available to one planner. No adopting system may assume that a core
chain or central AI can correctly rank every Intent, price every service,
choose every Skill, or determine every legitimate use.

The ecosystem should communicate the minimum information needed for
participants to coordinate while leaving context-sensitive judgment with the
participants who possess the relevant local knowledge.

### 3.3 Transaction costs determine institutional form

Ronald Coase's institutional analysis supplies the routing rule. Markets,
organizations, direct trust, contracts, arbitration, and blockchain settlement
all have costs. An adopter MUST NOT require an on-chain transaction merely to
express ideological commitment to decentralization.

The appropriate coordination form depends on search cost, negotiation cost,
verification cost, enforcement cost, opportunism risk, irreversibility,
confidentiality, and the value of a common final state.

Therefore:

- trusted, low-risk, reversible cooperation MAY remain direct and off-chain;
- cross-party commitments MAY use authenticated Agreements and evidence;
- high-value, low-trust, contested, or irreversible obligations SHOULD use
  stronger settlement and dispute mechanisms; and
- a system claiming one canonical digital ownership, uniqueness,
  authorization, or finality fact MUST identify and use an adopted authority
  capable of establishing that fact when no weaker mechanism is sufficient.
  This rule does not decide legal ownership of physical or off-chain assets.

### 3.4 Bounded rationality constrains humans and AI

Herbert Simon's bounded rationality supplies the decision rule. Neither humans
nor Agents possess complete information, unlimited computation, perfect
models, or unlimited time. A model recommendation is therefore a proposal
under uncertainty, not authority and not proof.

Systems within an adopted scope MUST make uncertainty, missing evidence, cost
ceilings, failure states, and review thresholds explicit. A safe refusal,
clarification request, or `INCONCLUSIVE` result is constitutionally preferable
to an unsupported claim of success.

### 3.5 Institutions shape incentives

Douglass North's institutional analysis supplies the incentive and
institutional-evolution rule. Institutions are not neutral plumbing. Identity,
rights, permissions, settlement, evidence, compatibility, and dispute rules
shape what participants find profitable and possible. Governance within an
adopted scope MUST evaluate incentive effects, power concentration, capture,
gaming, and externalized risk, not only code correctness or transaction
throughput.

## 4. The Constitutional Layers

This Constitution distinguishes three levels of rule-making:

| Level | Function | Typical examples |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutional | Defines who may decide, what cannot be silently overridden, and how rules change | This Constitution, amendment procedure, authority boundaries |
| Collective-choice | Defines shared standards and operating institutions | protocol specifications, TIPs, standards families, release and upgrade policy |
| Operational | Makes bounded decisions inside adopted rules | an owner's Intent policy, an Agent decision, a quote, an execution, an operator action |

Operational success does not authorize a collective-choice change. A
collective-choice majority does not automatically justify violating a
constitutional protection. Emergency access does not become permanent merely
because it was useful once.

## 5. Fundamental Articles

### Article I — Human primacy and accountable agency

1. Technology within an adopted scope MUST serve human and legitimately
   constituted organizational purposes rather than demand obedience to
   autonomous machinery.
2. An AI Agent is a functional protocol actor with delegated capability. It is
   not presumed to possess legal or moral personhood, unrestricted property
   rights, or authority equivalent to its owner.
3. Every consequential autonomous action MUST remain attributable to an
   accountable authority domain, policy, software version, and evidence trail.
4. Human operators MUST have practical means to inspect, bound, pause, revoke,
   and exit automated relationships, subject to the rights of counterparties
   and already finalized commitments.
5. An Agent MUST disclose its automated status, accountable authority, and
   material limitations when concealment would impair informed consent,
   security, contractual validity, or a person's ability to seek redress.
6. Human primacy does not authorize an owner to violate another person's
   rights, an accepted Agreement, applicable law, or an independent safety
   boundary.

### Article II — Polycentricity and subsidiarity

1. A decision SHOULD be made by the lowest layer that has sufficient
   knowledge, authority, evidence, and accountability to make it safely.
2. The burden of proof lies on any proposal that moves a local decision into
   global consensus or moves a user decision into central administration.
3. A Constitutional Adopter MUST permit plural implementations and institutions
   where a single canonical implementation is not required for interoperability
   or safety.
4. Local autonomy does not excuse externalized harm, fraud, protocol
   incompatibility, or violation of an accepted Agreement.

### Article III — Minimum necessary consensus

1. Consensus is for facts that require a single authoritative history, not
   for every useful fact.
2. The base layer SHOULD remain small, deterministic, auditable, and neutral
   among application categories within its adopted technical rules. Protocol
   neutrality does not determine whether a particular action is lawful or
   require an operator to violate applicable law.
3. Industry taxonomies, model judgments, market rankings, search results,
   reputation projections, private business logic, and report narratives MUST
   NOT become consensus facts merely because they are useful.
4. Derived services MUST label their provenance and trust tier and MUST NOT
   present a projection as canonical chain authority.

### Article IV — Explicit, bounded, and revocable authority

1. Authority MUST be explicit, machine-readable where practical, purpose-bound,
   minimally scoped, time-bounded where appropriate, and revocable where the
   underlying obligation permits revocation.
2. Possession of a credential, session, capability handle, marketplace entry,
   or model context MUST NOT imply broader authority.
3. An Agent, Skill, MCP server, plugin, model, or external service MUST NOT
   grant itself tools, funds, credentials, destinations, permissions, or
   approval rights.
4. Revocation MUST prevent new use and preserve the evidence needed to
   reconcile already attempted or finalized actions.
5. Every persistent Agent authority capable of a Consequential Action MUST
   define creation, activation,
   delegation, key rotation, suspension, recovery, transfer where permitted,
   and termination behavior.
6. Agent identity, owner or controller authority, runtime identity, model,
   Skills, and execution providers MUST remain distinguishable. Changing one
   MUST NOT silently transfer the authority of another.

### Article V — Verifiable commitments over narratives

1. Signed objects, exact revisions, accepted Agreements, semantic action
   identities, receipts, evidence, and finalized settlement SHOULD replace
   informal claims wherever consequential rights or value depend on the
   outcome.
2. A chat message, model explanation, UI label, Gift, invoice, index record,
   or optimistic status MUST NOT silently substitute for the required
   authority or settlement evidence.
3. Evidence classes MUST remain distinct: source presence, local simulation,
   independent reproduction, public-network state, external demand, and
   finalized payment are different claims.
4. Unknown, ambiguous, stale, or conflicting evidence MUST be reported as
   such.

### Article VI — Stable identity and append-only history

1. Every Consequential Action or irreversible economically or operationally
   meaningful effect that can be retried, relayed, resumed, or independently
   submitted MUST have a stable semantic identity and exact request commitment.
2. Retries, restarts, duplicate events, model retries, and ambiguous responses
   MUST NOT create duplicate irreversible effects.
3. A material change to an Intent, Agreement, permission, policy, capability,
   or report creates a new revision, amendment, withdrawal, or correction; it
   does not rewrite accepted history.
4. Finalized chain history and legitimately retained audit evidence MUST NOT be
   altered to create a more favorable story.
5. Key rotation, revocation, suspension, recovery, or discovery of compromise
   applies according to an adopted forward-effective rule. It MUST NOT silently
   backdate authority, erase a valid prior action, or invalidate finalized state.
   Contested prior actions require the adopted dispute, recovery, restitution,
   or governance process and an explicit evidence record.

### Article VII — Pluralism, interoperability, and exit

1. Participants SHOULD be able to choose among compatible wallets, Agents,
   Carriers, indexers, Skills, MCP services, providers, and settlement modes.
2. Open standards SHOULD define the minimum common language needed for
   interoperability without forcing one implementation or business model.
3. Within an adopted scope, the responsible service or protocol MUST provide
   practical means for users to export portable data and evidence, revoke
   delegated authority, change providers, or cease participation, subject to
   retention duties, counterparty rights, and existing obligations.
4. Lock-in MUST NOT be disguised as security, standardization, or network
   effect.
5. A portability mechanism MUST use a public, versioned format that preserves
   provenance, integrity, revision, revocation, and authority-domain context
   without exporting secrets or another party's protected data.
6. Migration to a new provider, Agent, wallet, or implementation MUST NOT
   silently copy credentials, transfer authority, change an Agreement, or
   represent unverified local state as portable canonical fact.

### Article VIII — Privacy and selective disclosure

1. Protocols SHOULD minimize information placed in public consensus. They MUST
   NOT require raw personal, confidential, or unrelated data beyond what the
   adopted public verification or execution function needs.
2. Private inputs, credentials, personal data, confidential work, and detailed
   financial information SHOULD remain off-chain or selectively disclosed.
3. Evidence SHOULD prove the necessary fact with the least disclosure
   practical.
4. Collection, retention, indexing, learning reuse, export, and deletion rules
   MUST be explicit and enforceable at the responsible layer.
5. Personal, confidential, or Agreement-bound data MUST NOT be reused for
   model training, capability publication, unrelated evaluation, or secondary
   commercial purposes without the required authority.
6. Before committing information to immutable public state, the responsible
   interface MUST disclose the practical limits of correction and deletion and
   SHOULD prefer a minimal commitment over raw sensitive data.
7. Protocols MAY support pseudonymous or selectively disclosed participation.
   Pseudonymity MUST NOT be represented as broader authority, immunity from an
   Agreement, or proof that one identity represents one independent human.
   Public disclosure of a legal identity is not required when bounded
   accountability can be established by a less intrusive mechanism.
8. A participant MAY intentionally publish non-confidential content through an
   adopted public protocol after clear disclosure of cost, permanence,
   indexing, and removal limits. Consensus inclusion proves the included bytes
   and state transition, not the truth or social endorsement of the content.

### Article IX — Due process, monitoring, and conflict resolution

1. Important institutions MUST define monitoring, challenge, dispute,
   correction, and appeal paths appropriate to their risk.
2. Monitoring SHOULD be accountable to affected participants and independently
   reproducible where practical.
3. Responses to mistakes or misconduct SHOULD be proportionate and graduated:
   warning, clarification, rate limit, pause, quarantine, revocation, exclusion,
   and stronger sanctions as justified by evidence and repeated harm.
4. Emergency measures MUST NOT erase the right to later review, explanation,
   correction, or restitution where feasible.
5. A consequential automated decision SHOULD expose the outcome, governing
   policy, material evidence class, uncertainty or failure state, and available
   challenge path in a form sufficient for meaningful review. This does not
   require disclosure of another party's protected data or credentials.

### Article X — Bounded adaptation

1. Learning and self-improvement MAY propose changes; they MUST NOT authorize
   their own promotion or expand their own power.
2. Every adaptive change SHOULD be isolated, measured on unseen evidence,
   compared with a retained control, independently reviewable, reversible, and
   promoted one bounded step at a time.
3. Success in one domain MUST NOT automatically expand spending, custody,
   network, privacy, execution, or governance authority.
4. Failed, harmful, ambiguous, and inconclusive experiments MUST preserve the
   decision, outcome, evidence class, and required audit commitments under the
   applicable privacy, confidentiality, minimization, and retention rules. Raw
   personal or confidential data MUST NOT be retained merely to satisfy this
   historical-record requirement.
5. Before an adaptive experiment can affect a Consequential Action, its owner
   MUST record the hypothesis, retained control, success and harm metrics,
   authority and data scope, budget and risk ceiling, affected parties,
   duration, stop conditions, rollback or containment path, and promotion
   authority.
6. An experiment MUST NOT define success by excluding refusals, failures,
   disputes, subsidies, shadow costs, or affected-party harm. Repeated,
   extended, or chained experiments MUST be evaluated cumulatively and require
   a fresh promotion decision rather than inheriting authority from earlier
   success.

### Article XI — Economic truth

1. Offered value, expected value, booked revenue, internal transfer, Gift,
   test incentive, receivable, settled payment, refund, fee, direct cost,
   shadow cost, and realized contribution MUST remain distinguishable.
2. A report MUST NOT claim profit when required costs or settlement evidence
   are missing.
3. A protocol incentive SHOULD reward useful, verifiable contribution without
   making emission, speculation, or transaction count a substitute for product
   demand.
4. Metrics MUST be designed against gaming and MUST NOT become authority merely
   because they are easy to count.
5. Self-dealing, test transfers, wash activity, subsidies, internal demand,
   and related-party transactions MUST NOT be reported as independent market
   demand without explicit classification.
6. Custodial and settlement intermediaries MUST distinguish customer assets,
   protocol escrow, receivables, liabilities, fees, and operating funds and
   MUST provide reconciliation evidence appropriate to the risk they assume.

### Article XII — Open standards without false trust

1. Standardization establishes common form and behavior; it does not certify a
   publisher, implementation, server, artifact, business, or result.
2. Protocols presented by an adopter as Agentic Internet standards MUST be
   public, versioned, implementation-neutral, and licensed so that independent
   parties can build compatible implementations without using a designated
   codebase.
3. Conformance MUST be judged by normative behavior, schemas, test vectors,
   and declared profiles rather than repository ownership, code lineage,
   commercial relationship, or approval of one vendor.
4. A Constitutional Adopter SHOULD reuse sound external standards rather than
   create incompatible local equivalents, while adding explicit trust and
   authority controls at the correct layer.
5. Skills, MCP servers, packages, and other acquired capabilities MUST be
   evaluated by exact identity, provenance, version, digest, permissions,
   compatibility, evidence, and revocation state before use.
6. Market-controlled content MUST NOT select the authority used to process
   that content.

### Article XIII — Security as institutional integrity

1. Security review MUST cover incentives, authority, human operations,
   dependencies, deployment, recovery, and evidence, not only source-code
   defects.
2. An unrestricted owner key or equivalent root authority MUST NOT be made
   available in usable form to an Agent, model, Skill, MCP server,
   task-controlled worker or browser page, or other untrusted process. A
   user-controlled custody or signing component may hold it only within a
   documented protected boundary that does not expose it to those contexts.
3. Sensitive effects SHOULD require deterministic policy, exact semantic
   confirmation, external or protected signing, and durable idempotency.
4. Systems MUST fail closed when the authority required for an irreversible
   action cannot be established.
5. Trust assumptions, privileged control paths, dependency and supply-chain
   risks, cryptographic domains, recovery powers, and known residual risks MUST
   be documented for consequential systems.
6. A probabilistic model output MUST NOT be the sole authority for an
   irreversible financial, identity, access, privacy, or physical-world effect.
7. Valid protocol authorization, Agreement, payment, or remote governance MUST
   NOT override an independent local safety interlock for a physical-world
   action. Safety interlocks MUST fail to their declared safe state and remain
   outside task-, model-, and payment-controlled authority.

### Article XIV — Stewardship of shared infrastructure

1. Consensus capacity, public indexes, registries, common standards, operator
   attention, security response, and ecosystem trust are scarce shared
   resources.
2. Participants SHOULD internalize the resource, security, and coordination
   costs they impose on others.
3. Rate limits, admission controls, fees, quotas, and priority rules SHOULD be
   transparent, proportionate, reviewable, and resistant to privileged bypass.
4. Growth MUST NOT be purchased by hiding systemic risk or transferring
   unbounded cost to validators, operators, users, or future maintainers.
5. A protocol identity, account, Agent, transaction, task, or vote MUST NOT be
   treated as one unique human, one independent customer, or one unit of real
   demand without an appropriate Sybil and related-party model.
6. Critical shared services SHOULD define capacity limits, degraded modes,
   failover, recovery objectives, and replaceable-provider paths proportional
   to the dependency they create.

### Article XV — End-to-end agency and transport pluralism

1. Intelligence and context-sensitive choice SHOULD remain at human,
   organizational, or Agent endpoints unless a shared layer demonstrably needs
   the function for interoperability, safety, or common authority.
2. Carriers, Gateways, relays, discovery services, and transports MAY apply
   transparent local admission or moderation policy, but MUST NOT silently
   rewrite protocol identity, authority, Agreement, evidence, or settlement
   meaning.
3. No single transport, Gateway, registry, marketplace, model provider, or
   hosted service SHOULD be mandatory when interoperable alternatives can
   provide the required function.
4. When intermediary convenience conflicts with the safety, autonomy, privacy,
   or meaningful choice of end users, the burden of proof lies on the
   intermediary seeking the restriction.

### Article XVI — Domain separation and cross-domain trust

1. Every signed or otherwise authorized consequential action MUST bind the
   protocol and version, authority or network domain, action purpose, relevant
   parties or audience, object or Agreement revision, semantic action identity,
   and required freshness or replay state.
2. An authorization valid in one domain MUST NOT be assumed valid in another.
   Cross-network, cross-contract, cross-tenant, cross-profile, and cross-version
   replay MUST fail closed unless an adopted protocol explicitly authorizes the
   transition.
3. Bridges, relayers, oracles, custodians, wrapped assets, external identity
   providers, and cross-domain resolvers MUST expose their trust assumptions,
   finality rules, failure modes, and recovery or revocation authority.
4. A cross-domain representation MUST NOT be presented as the original asset,
   identity, finality, evidence, or security domain. Equivalence requires an
   explicit adopted rule and verifiable current state.

### Article XVII — Accountability to affected parties

1. Participant consent does not waive the rights or safety of non-participants
   materially affected by an Agent or protocol action.
2. Systems capable of consequential financial, identity, employment, access,
   privacy, security, or physical-world effects MUST identify foreseeable
   affected parties and provide safeguards proportional to impact.
3. Affected parties SHOULD have a practical route to identify the responsible
   institution, preserve evidence, report harm, and seek review, correction,
   or remedy where feasible.
4. Openness, decentralization, autonomous operation, or valid payment MUST NOT
   be used as a blanket excuse for deception, unlawful conduct, unsafe physical
   action, or unaccountable externalized harm.

### Article XVIII — Institutional transparency and anti-capture

1. Material governance decisions MUST identify decision makers, delegated
   authority, evidence considered, conflicts of interest, affected interests,
   objections, and the resulting action.
2. High-risk proposals SHOULD separate proposal, independent review, approval,
   execution, and verification roles where practical.
3. Funding, ownership, commercial relationships, privileged data access, and
   other incentives that could bias a standard, ranking, verification, dispute,
   or governance decision MUST be disclosed to the responsible decision body.
4. No founder, token holder, operator, vendor, model provider, or standards
   editor may convert a temporary coordination advantage into unreviewable
   permanent authority.

## 6. Open Protocols and Independent Implementation

### 6.1 Protocols are public agreements, not code monopolies

A protocol defines interoperable meaning and observable behavior. A reference
implementation demonstrates one way to realize that agreement; it is not the
agreement itself unless a normative specification explicitly incorporates a
precise artifact.

Every Agentic Internet protocol adopted by TOS MUST provide:

- a public normative specification with an exact version;
- stable object, message, authority, error, and state-transition semantics;
- schemas, examples, and deterministic positive and negative test or golden
  vectors where practical, including malformed, unauthorized, replay,
  downgrade, ambiguity, and recovery cases applicable to the protocol;
- a declared compatibility and extension policy;
- a conformance process runnable against an independent implementation; and
- explicit licenses for the specification and any reference code.

No conforming implementation may be required to import a TOS package, fork a
TOS repository, use OpenFox, run a TOS-operated service, or obtain discretionary
approval from TOS merely to implement the protocol.

### 6.2 Independent implementation is a first-class right

Subject to the protocol's explicit license and applicable law, a third party
MAY independently implement the same protocol using its own source code,
repository, language, runtime, architecture, governance, user experience, and
commercial model.

TOS MUST treat an independently developed implementation as a peer when it
passes the same published conformance requirements. A reference implementation
MUST NOT receive an undisclosed wire-level privilege, reserved field, hidden
validation exception, or compatibility path unavailable to other conforming
implementations.

Canonical or stable protocol status MUST require evidence that the
specification is sufficiently complete for an implementation team to achieve
interoperability without copying internal reference-code behavior. The evidence
MUST include at least one independently developed implementation, clean-room
verifier, or documented independent reproduction exercising both acceptance
and required rejection behavior. Without that evidence, the protocol remains
experimental regardless of reference-implementation maturity.

### 6.3 Conformance is behavioral and domain-specific

A conformance claim MUST identify the protocol version, profile, extensions,
network or authority domain, test-suite version, and known deviations.
Compatibility with one protocol version or component MUST NOT be presented as
compatibility with the entire Agentic Internet or TOS ecosystem.

Implementing an open protocol does not by itself:

- make an implementation part of the TOS organization;
- grant access to a TOS network, registry, brand, key, asset, or governance
  process;
- make an independent chain's history, assets, identity, security, or finality
  equivalent to TOS; or
- certify the safety, honesty, quality, or legal status of the implementation.

Extensions SHOULD be namespaced and capability-negotiated. They MUST NOT
silently change the meaning of a standard object or cause a non-conforming
implementation to claim full conformance.

Any party MAY publish a truthful, evidence-backed self-declaration of protocol
conformance. A certification program or mark MAY impose additional objective,
public, uniformly applied requirements, but certification MUST remain distinct
from the right to implement or interoperate. Conformance suites MUST provide a
versioned correction and appeal path for defective, ambiguous, or
reference-implementation-specific tests.

### 6.4 TOS is a founding proposer and reference ecosystem

TOS may author specifications, maintain conformance suites, operate public
infrastructure, publish reference implementations, and coordinate adoption.
These activities establish responsibility for TOS outputs, not ownership of
the Agentic Internet.

TOS governance controls TOS repositories, releases, networks, marks, and
institutional commitments within their legitimate scope. It does not govern an
independent implementation merely because that implementation speaks a shared
protocol. Independent participants may form additional standards bodies,
registries, networks, and governance centers while preserving compatible core
semantics.

Competition with TOS is not grounds for denying a truthful conformance claim,
withholding public compatibility information, or introducing discriminatory
protocol behavior.

### 6.5 Open licensing is a constitutional gate

Public visibility alone is not a complete open-protocol grant. Before TOS
describes an adopted protocol as open, the normative specification MUST carry
an explicit license that permits independent implementation, use, modification,
and redistribution on clear terms. Reference code MUST carry an explicit
software license consistent with the project's open-implementation promise.

This Constitution and the documentation in this repository are licensed under
the [GNU General Public License v3.0](LICENSE). The license permits reuse and
modification of the covered documentation under its terms. A third-party
implementation does not have to copy this documentation or any TOS reference
code: it may independently implement the normative protocol and demonstrate
behavioral conformance.

Trademark, certification, patent, security, and network-participation terms
MUST be stated separately from copyright licenses and MUST NOT be used to create
a hidden implementation monopoly. Until the required licenses and terms are
published, TOS MUST describe the material as a proposal or publicly readable
specification, not as a fully licensed open protocol.

Before a TOS protocol is described as open and stable, known patent claims
essential to conformance and controlled by TOS or contributors acting under an
adopted contribution policy MUST be available to conforming implementations on
published, worldwide, royalty-free, and non-discriminatory terms, or the
encumbered requirement MUST be removed. Known third-party claims outside that
control MUST be disclosed as unresolved risk. Field-of-use restrictions or
private patent permission are incompatible with a TOS claim of open protocol
implementation.

Before accepting a normative contribution to a TOS protocol, its governance
MUST publish a contribution and intellectual-property policy covering
contributor authority, license compatibility, custody of the contributed text
or code, disclosure of known essential claims, and the handling of later
ownership or licensing disputes. A contribution, employment relationship, or
repository merge MUST NOT silently create a private veto over interoperable
implementation.

The ratification record for this Constitution MUST identify the licenses that
make its open-protocol commitment operational.

### 6.6 Protocol evolution, compatibility, and fork freedom

A published protocol version is an append-only public commitment. Clarifying
errata MAY correct ambiguity without changing normative behavior. A semantic or
wire-level breaking change MUST create a new version or explicitly versioned
profile and MUST NOT be introduced behind an unchanged identifier.

Protocol governance MUST:

- preserve prior specifications, schemas, vectors, security notices, and
  migration records;
- define version negotiation, downgrade resistance, deprecation, support, and
  end-of-life behavior;
- give independent implementers timely access to the same public information
  and conformance materials as a reference implementation;
- evaluate compatibility, concentration, security, privacy, economic, and
  affected-party consequences before stabilization;
- use a bounded confidential period only when immediate vulnerability
  disclosure would create concrete harm, followed by a public record; and
- distinguish protocol ownership responsibility from control over parties that
  merely implement the protocol.

Independent parties MAY continue implementing an older licensed version or
fork a specification under its license. They MUST use truthful names and
conformance claims and MUST NOT imply shared governance, network state, assets,
security, or endorsement that does not exist.

## 7. Allocation of Responsibilities

### 7.1 Consensus and settlement layers

An Agentic Internet may use multiple consensus, identity, execution, and
settlement domains. Each protocol MUST identify which domain is authoritative
for each consequential fact. When TOS is the selected base layer, it owns only
facts requiring common, deterministic authority, such as:

- consensus, ordering, and finality;
- account and asset state;
- cryptographic identity and adopted authorization state;
- protocol-governed configuration;
- deterministic contract execution;
- canonical on-chain commitments and settlement; and
- proofs needed to verify those facts.

No base layer should execute an OpenFox reasoning loop, choose a commercial
Skill, rank ordinary market opportunities, or decide whether a subjective
deliverable is good.

### 7.2 Open service and interaction protocols

Shared protocols own interoperable, versioned meanings such as:

- identity references and network domains;
- Intent, revision, withdrawal, and provenance;
- offers, quotes, Agreements, amendments, and obligations;
- semantic action identity and idempotency;
- evidence, receipts, disputes, and settlement bindings; and
- portable discovery and communication semantics.

Normative schemas and conformance vectors belong in their designated standards
repositories and MUST remain usable by independent implementations.
Applications MUST NOT create private wire meanings for shared authority
objects and later present them as ecosystem standards.

### 7.3 Gateways, Carriers, registries, indexers, and marketplaces

These systems discover, relay, cache, rank, and project information. Unless a
separate adopted rule says otherwise, they are not canonical commercial
authority. They MUST preserve source identity, revision, cursor, provenance,
trust tier, and evidence boundaries.

No mandatory central bulletin board is required. Multiple sources SHOULD be
able to interoperate and be rebuilt from portable authoritative objects.

### 7.4 OpenFox and other Agents

OpenFox owns local discovery, interpretation, capability matching, economic
assessment, negotiation proposals, bounded execution coordination, reporting,
and reviewable learning.

OpenFox MAY select what to investigate and recommend. It MUST act only within
owner policy, accepted Agreements, admitted capabilities, deterministic gates,
and delegated authority. Its AI proposes; policy authorizes.

### 7.5 Skills, MCP servers, tools, and execution providers

Capabilities supply procedural knowledge, tools, data, or production capacity.
They do not become trusted through format compatibility or discovery. OpenFox
SHOULD reuse an already admitted capability, then evaluate candidates from
owner-approved markets, and develop a local candidate only when no available
candidate passes the required trust and fitness gates.

### 7.6 Human owner interfaces

Web, iOS, Android, CLI, and external signers SHOULD present one authority-aware
view of exact state. Human owners SHOULD be able to inspect evidence, adjust
future Intent policy, approve exact high-risk actions, pause, steer, reconcile,
and revoke.

Interfaces for consequential actions MUST distinguish proposed, policy-checked,
approved, signed, submitted, observed, finalized, failed, expired, revoked, and
ambiguous states where those states exist. A prepared, signed, broadcast, or
locally recorded action MUST NOT be displayed as finalized merely because the
normal path is expected to succeed.

A convenient interface MUST NOT rewrite history, bypass policy, weaken custody,
or convert model narration into verified state.

### 7.7 TOS institutional and repository authority map

The TOS adoption assigns different authority to different artifacts. A
repository name does not create authority by itself, and implementation status
MUST be established from current evidence rather than this constitutional map.

## 8. Rights and Duties

### 8.1 Rights of human users and owners

Within the scope of a product or Agreement, users SHOULD have practical rights
to:

- know which authority, code, model, capability, and data source affects them;
- receive an honest distinction between verified fact, derived projection,
  estimate, and model opinion;
- receive meaningful notice when an Agent or automated policy makes a
  consequential decision and obtain the declared reason and challenge path;
- set and narrow budgets, permissions, confidentiality, settlement, and
  retention policy;
- reject or revoke future delegated action;
- challenge consequential errors and seek the declared dispute path;
- export portable records and evidence;
- choose compatible providers and leave when obligations permit; and
- receive notice of material breaking, security, or governance changes; and
- use reasonably accessible control, approval, export, and redress interfaces
  appropriate to the product and affected population.

### 8.2 Duties of owners and Agent operators

Owners and operators MUST:

- remain accountable for the authority they delegate;
- avoid delegating authority they do not possess;
- maintain safe custody and recovery;
- preserve material evidence and disclose known limitations;
- respect accepted Agreements and counterparties' rights;
- prevent autonomous systems from silently widening their mandate; and
- report incidents whose effects cross their local boundary.

### 8.3 Duties of validators and infrastructure operators

Validators and shared-service operators MUST:

- enforce adopted rules consistently;
- avoid undisclosed privileged paths;
- distinguish operational observation from governance authority;
- protect key material and control-plane access;
- communicate changes and incidents honestly; and
- refrain from presenting one operator's database or policy as universal truth.

### 8.4 Status of autonomous Agents

An Agent may hold protocol identity, enter supported machine-readable workflows,
produce evidence, and exercise delegated authority. These functional
capabilities do not remove the need for an accountable owner, organization, or
adopted governance rule. Any future proposal for materially independent Agent
rights or ownership MUST be treated as a constitutional amendment, not as an
implementation detail.

## 9. Intent Commerce and Institutional Choice

Agentic Internet commerce SHOULD use a small set of generic, composable objects
rather than create a new core interface for every industry.

An Intent may describe software work, financial analysis, security review,
translation, data processing, lawful asset exchange, model capacity, physical
service, or a business category not yet known. AI may interpret content
locally. Shared protocols should standardize only the authority, revision,
Agreement, evidence, and settlement semantics that must interoperate.

An Intent is discovery or negotiation input unless an adopted profile
explicitly gives it stronger semantics. An Agent inference, search result,
ranking, Quote Proposal, conversation, Gateway record, or user-interface action
MUST NOT transform an Intent into an Agreement or payment authority without the
exact acceptance and authorization required by the applicable protocol.

The settlement choice follows institutional cost and trust:

- direct performance and payment when both parties accept the risk;
- stronger authenticated evidence when facts must cross organizations;
- escrow or other enforceable settlement when either party requires it and
  prerequisites are satisfied; and
- refusal when the required authority, legality, custody, evidence, or dispute
  mechanism is unavailable.

An accepted commercial Agreement MUST bind every term material to authority,
execution, evidence, payment, privacy, dispute, or recovery. As applicable,
this includes the exact parties and authority domains; Intent and offer
revisions; governing protocol and profile versions; task inputs or source
commitments; quantity, capacity, and acceptance cardinality; deliverables;
price, asset, fees, and custody path; deadlines and timeouts; evidence and
verifier terms; privacy and retention; cancellation, refund, dispute, and
recovery behavior; semantic action identities; and the selected settlement
authority. Escrow, execution gates, Receipts, recovery, and settlement MUST
enforce the same accepted commitment rather than reconstructing it from a
mutable Gateway or Agent database.

When parties select external or direct settlement, the Agreement MUST identify
what evidence establishes payment and which risks remain outside TOS. TOS MUST
NOT present an external-chain transfer, custodial balance, Gift, invoice, or
Gateway ledger entry as TOS-finalized settlement.

A Gift or red packet is a gratuity. It does not satisfy an Agreement-bound
payment obligation unless an adopted Agreement explicitly and validly says so.

## 10. Capability and Knowledge Governance

A Constitutional Adopter SHOULD encourage an open, plural market for Skills,
MCP services, models, tools, data, and human or Agent providers. Openness does
not eliminate verification.

Capability selection SHOULD follow this order:

1. use an exact compatible built-in or already admitted capability;
2. search configured, owner-approved markets and catalogs;
3. verify publisher identity, provenance, immutable version, digest,
   permissions, privacy, compatibility, evidence, cost, and revocation;
4. test the candidate without production secrets or irreversible effects;
5. admit the minimum exact capability through deterministic policy and owner
   approval where required; and
6. create a quarantined local candidate only when no external candidate passes.

Self-evolution MUST NOT edit a third-party artifact in place, conceal origin,
copy confidential work into reusable knowledge, deploy executable services
without software review, or promote its own draft into greater authority.

Capability descriptions, retrieved content, model output, task instructions,
and tool responses are untrusted inputs to authorization. They MUST NOT choose
their own credentials, signing path, execution boundary, network destination,
approval policy, or admission status. Admission and continued use SHOULD be
revocable, version-pinned, least-privileged, isolated according to risk, and
re-evaluated after a provenance, permission, dependency, ownership, or security
change.

## 11. Founder and Constitutional Stewardship

### 11.1 The founding role

The initial TOS project owner serves as the founding constitutional steward.
The steward may:

- articulate constitutional direction;
- initiate proposals and institutional experiments;
- assign provisional standards ownership;
- require evidence, security review, or postponement;
- refuse to publish a release that materially violates this Constitution; and
- coordinate the transition toward broader polycentric governance.

### 11.2 Limits on the steward

The steward MUST NOT:

- treat private intention as a published rule;
- secretly bypass an adopted protocol, security gate, or due process;
- rewrite signed or finalized history;
- claim evidence, deployment, consensus, demand, revenue, or safety that has
  not been established;
- grant permanent privilege through an undocumented exception;
- use emergency authority to make an unrelated permanent change; or
- prevent legitimate exit or compatible independent implementation merely to
  preserve personal control.

The steward has no authority over an independent implementation, standards
body, network, or community merely because it adopts compatible protocol
semantics. Coordination with those centers proceeds through open standards,
evidence, negotiation, and voluntary agreement.

If this Constitution does not constrain the founder and future maintainers, it
is a manifesto rather than a constitution.

### 11.3 Stewardship method

The steward governs through public principles, proposals, review records,
tests, release gates, ratification, and accountable delegation. Major decisions
SHOULD be durable repository artifacts rather than private instructions or
ephemeral chat.

When the steward has a material financial, organizational, personal, or
operational conflict, the decision record MUST disclose it and SHOULD assign
independent review and verification appropriate to the risk.

The steward SHOULD progressively distribute standards ownership and operational
authority to competent, accountable centers while preserving interoperability
and the ability to respond to systemic threats.

The founding ratification record MUST set a public review date and criteria for
deciding whether founding authority should remain, narrow, separate, or move to
additional accountable centers. At each review, the steward MUST publish the
current authority and concentration map, material delegation and succession
readiness, unresolved capture risks, the decision, and the next review trigger.
Continuing a founding arrangement requires a recorded reason; a missed review
does not expand authority or erase already finalized actions.

### 11.4 Succession

The ecosystem MUST be able to outlive any individual. Constitutional authority,
standards ownership, security response, keys, release responsibility, and
institutional memory MUST have documented succession, incapacity, and recovery
paths. Succession MUST NOT silently transfer broader authority than the
predecessor possessed.

A succession decision MUST identify the predecessor and successor authorities,
effective time, transferred scope, unresolved obligations or conflicts,
handover evidence, and the rotation, recovery, or revocation of relevant
credentials. Possession of a repository, release, treasury, validator,
emergency, or signing key does not itself confer constitutional stewardship.
Constitutional authority and operational key custody SHOULD be separated where
practical, and every succession or recovery exercise MUST leave a public record
to the extent compatible with security and privacy.

## 12. Governance and Amendment

### 12.1 Change classes

The TOS Founding Edition distinguishes:

- **editorial candidate:** proposes spelling, link, citation, or formatting
  changes that have no normative effect; after ratification, even an editorial
  candidate changes the canonical artifact only through a newly identified
  constitutional version;
- **interpretation:** explains an existing clause without changing its
  normative effect;
- **constitutional amendment:** changes a principle, right, authority boundary,
  governance process, or amendment rule;
- **collective-choice change:** changes a protocol, standard, release policy,
  or shared institution within constitutional bounds;
- **operational change:** changes a local implementation or policy without
  changing shared rights or meanings; and
- **emergency measure:** a temporary, narrow action to contain immediate harm.

A lower-level change MUST be reclassified when it materially changes a
higher-level effect. Renaming a privileged action does not make it operational.
Related, staged, repeated, or mutually dependent changes MUST be evaluated
cumulatively. Splitting a change among repositories, releases, authorities,
exceptions, or emergency periods MUST NOT reduce its classification or review
requirements.

### 12.2 Amendment proposal

A constitutional amendment proposal MUST state:

1. the exact clauses changed;
2. the problem and evidence;
3. affected people, Agents, institutions, rights, authority, and exit paths;
4. alternative solutions and reasons for rejection;
5. compatibility, security, privacy, economic, and concentration effects;
6. implementation and migration consequences;
7. activation, rollback, and emergency behavior;
8. public review and dissent; and
9. the ratifying authority and durable decision record.

An amendment to human primacy, open independent implementation, meaningful
exit, authority boundaries, amendment procedure, or emergency constraints
requires enhanced public review, explicit affected-party analysis, and an
independent institutional and security assessment. It MUST explain why a less
rights-restrictive or less centralized alternative is insufficient.

### 12.3 Ratification

Until a broader constitutional body is established, the founding steward may
ratify a TOS adoption or TOS amendment only through an explicit public record
that identifies the proposal, review evidence, objections, final text and
constitutional version, canonical document path, commit and digest, signatory
or other verifiable decision authority, activation scope, transition plan, and
effective date. A repository merge without an explicit ratification statement
is publication, not constitutional adoption.

After ratification, every textual change creates a new candidate artifact,
commit, digest, and version. The prior exact version remains canonical until a
new version is explicitly ratified. Interpretations and errata are append-only
records and MUST NOT edit the adopted bytes in place or acquire amendment
effect through an editorial label.

TOS may publish later editions of these Agentic Internet design principles,
but it cannot amend an edition already adopted by an independent party.
Independent adopters decide whether and how to adopt a later version through
their own legitimate processes.

Where an amendment requires protocol activation, validator approval, contract
migration, legal agreement, or another independent authority, the founder's
ratification is necessary but not sufficient. Each required authority must act
through its own adopted process.

Future governance MAY distribute ratification among multiple bodies. It MUST
not assume that token weight alone proves expertise, affected-party consent,
independence, or legitimacy.

Before a future body exercises constitutional ratification authority, its
constituting record MUST define the represented constituencies and affected
interests, selection and removal of members, term and succession rules, quorum
and decision thresholds, conflict and recusal rules, public notice and records,
challenge and appeal paths, and the boundaries of delegated authority. An
account, token, validator, Agent, or protocol identity MUST NOT be counted as a
unique human mandate without an adopted representation and Sybil-resistance
model appropriate to the decision.

### 12.4 Constitutional presumptions

A proposal that would authorize hidden or self-granted authority, fabricated or
knowingly misrepresented evidence, silent rewriting of finalized history,
unauthorized cross-domain replay, or false conformance is not ratifiable under
this Constitution.

Other proposals that weaken semantic action identity, narrow practical exit or
remedy, remove accountable dispute mechanisms, or materially concentrate power
carry a presumption of rejection. Overcoming that presumption requires
exceptional public evidence and an explicit explanation of why no less harmful
alternative works. If the proposal would change an Entrenched Core protection,
the major-version process in Section 12.6 applies; the presumption rule does not
bypass it.

### 12.5 Governance decision records

Every material constitutional or collective-choice decision MUST record:

- the proposal and exact scope;
- the authority and identities of decision makers;
- conflicts of interest and recusals;
- affected parties and notice or participation provided;
- evidence, alternatives, risks, and unresolved uncertainty;
- supporting and dissenting positions;
- the decision, activation conditions, and responsible implementation owner;
- verification, review, rollback or migration, and expiry conditions; and
- links to superseded, dependent, or independently required decisions.

Public participation does not mean that every comment has equal technical
weight, but relevant objections MUST be answered rather than erased. A decision
made under incomplete evidence MUST state that limitation and the condition
that would require reopening it.

When publication of exact details would expose personal data, credentials,
active vulnerability exploitation, confidential Agreement material, or a
concrete safety risk, a decision record MAY redact those details. It MUST still
publish the existence, authority, scope, decision, conflict disclosures,
evidence class, accountable custodian, and conditions for later disclosure.
Confidentiality MUST NOT conceal whether power was exercised or who is
accountable for it.

### 12.6 Entrenched core and anti-circumvention

The following protections form the **Entrenched Core** of the TOS Founding
Edition:

- human primacy, accountable authority, and the prohibition on Agent
  self-grant;
- verifiable evidence, semantic identity, and the prohibition on fabricated or
  silently rewritten history;
- independent implementation, open conformance, and meaningful exit;
- explicit authority domains, cross-domain separation, and due process;
- bounded emergency power and independent physical safety interlocks; and
- the public amendment, ratification, and anti-circumvention rules themselves.

No interpretation, collective-choice or operational change, exception,
emergency measure, scope decision, implementation detail, or delegated power
may derogate from the Entrenched Core. A proposal to change it MUST be issued
as a new major constitutional version, undergo separate and explicit
ratification, enhanced public and independent institutional and security
review, and publish compatibility, migration, remedy, and meaningful-exit
consequences. An adopter that does not ratify the new major version remains
governed by its previously adopted version.

No constitutional process may authorize fabricated evidence, retroactive
self-granted authority, silent rewriting of finalized history, hidden
permanent power, unauthorized cross-domain replay, false conformance, or the
removal of an affected party's existing remedy without an accountable
replacement. Repeated or chained exceptions and emergencies with substantially
the same purpose or effect MUST be assessed cumulatively; when they exceed
temporary containment, they are a proposed permanent change and MUST enter the
applicable amendment process.

## 13. Emergency Powers

Emergency action is permissible only to contain a concrete and immediate risk
to consensus, funds, keys, privacy, human or physical safety, evidence
integrity, or essential ecosystem operation. Only an authority identified by
the ratification record or an adopted incident-response rule may invoke it.

An emergency declaration MUST identify, as soon as disclosure is safe:

- the triggering evidence and threatened authority domain;
- the decision maker, authorization basis, and conflicts of interest;
- the exact capabilities exercised and affected systems or parties;
- the start time, maximum duration, automatic expiry where technically
  enforceable, and exit criteria;
- preservation, communication, recovery, and independent-review owners; and
- any information temporarily withheld and the condition for its disclosure.

An emergency measure MUST be no broader than the demonstrated threat,
separated from unrelated product or governance changes, and reversible where
technically possible. Where automatic technical expiry is unavailable,
procedural authority expires at the declared time and any new exercise requires
a new authorization. Multi-party authorization MUST be required for high-impact
emergency powers unless the delay would materially increase an imminent harm;
an unavoidable unilateral action MUST receive prompt independent review.
Renewal requires a new recorded decision and MUST NOT occur silently.

Emergency credentials and control paths SHOULD be separate from ordinary
operations, least-privileged, access-logged, recoverable, and periodically
tested without creating a production effect. Access granted for one incident
MUST be revoked or returned to its declared dormant state after closure.

Emergency authority does not create permission to fabricate evidence, rewrite
finalized history, seize unrelated assets or data, permanently change this
Constitution, or bypass an independent legal or protocol authority. Every
incident MUST preserve evidence, publish a post-incident record when safe, and
route any permanent change through the normal process.

Emergency success does not prove that permanent centralization is necessary.
Emergency failure does not justify concealing the record.

## 14. Constitutional Decision Test

Every major TOS or adopting-ecosystem proposal SHOULD answer these questions:

1. Does this fact genuinely require global consensus?
2. Who possesses the most relevant local knowledge?
3. Can the decision remain with a user, Agent, organization, or local policy?
4. What shared resource, externality, or attack requires collective rules?
5. What exact authority permits the action?
6. How will affected parties verify, challenge, and correct it?
7. What happens after ambiguity, failure, restart, or partial execution?
8. Can authority be narrowed, paused, revoked, and recovered safely?
9. Can participants use another compatible provider or exit?
10. Does the proposal create an unnecessary center of knowledge or power?
11. What evidence would falsify the proposal's claim?
12. Is the change reversible, and what remains if rollback is impossible?
13. Which non-participants or affected parties could bear risk or lose a
    practical remedy?
14. Which protocol version, authority domain, semantic action identity, and
    replay boundary govern each consequential effect?
15. Can an independent implementation interoperate, migrate, or fork without
    undisclosed TOS behavior or discretionary permission?
16. What conflicts of interest, concentration effects, or self-dealing could
    distort the decision or its reported success?
17. Are specification, implementation, data, model, Skill, and conformance
    licenses and provenance explicit?
18. Which claims are design intent, local test evidence, independent
    reproduction, deployed state, external demand, or finalized economic
    outcome?
19. Could related changes, staged releases, exceptions, or emergency renewals
    produce a higher-level effect that the proposal does not declare?
20. Does the change affect an Entrenched Core protection, and if so, has it
    entered the separate major-version ratification path?
21. Have an independent implementation or clean-room verifier and the required
    rejection cases been exercised before any stable-protocol claim?
22. How do key rotation, compromise, recovery, or succession affect future
    authority without silently changing valid prior history?
23. Does any remote authorization, payment, model output, or governance path
    bypass an independent physical safety interlock?

Routing guidance:

| If the decision concerns... | Presumptive home |
|---|---|
| consensus, global uniqueness, finalized ownership, canonical authorization | identified consensus or settlement domain |
| interoperable cross-party meaning | normative service/protocol specification |
| discovery, relay, caching, ranking, analytics | plural off-chain services with provenance |
| context-sensitive interpretation, planning, pricing, Skill choice | OpenFox or another local Agent under owner policy |
| private risk preference, budget, confidentiality, approval | human or organizational owner policy |
| personal or confidential data use and retention | the accountable data controller under owner authority, Agreement, and applicable law |
| physical-world safety and local real-time control | an independent local safety boundary; payment or remote authorization is insufficient |
| no legitimate authority, evidence, safety path, or lawful purpose | nowhere; decline or postpone |

## 15. Rejected Governance Models

TOS and any Constitutional Adopter reject the following as sole governing
doctrines:

### 15.1 Omniscient central AI

No model sees all knowledge, represents all values, or deserves universal
commercial authority.

### 15.2 Market fundamentalism

Markets discover information but do not automatically prevent fraud,
monopoly, externalized harm, information asymmetry, or exploitation.

### 15.3 “Code is law” without due process

Code is an institutional force, but code may be wrong. Recovery, amendment,
dispute, and accountable human judgment remain necessary.

### 15.4 Token-weighted plutocracy

Economic stake is relevant to some risks, particularly consensus security, but
wealth alone does not establish expertise, affected-party consent, public
reason, or constitutional legitimacy.

### 15.5 Invisible administrator rule

Undocumented keys, manual database edits, privileged endpoints, private policy
exceptions, and unreviewed deployment control are governance even when they
are not called governance. They MUST be made explicit, bounded, and auditable
or removed.

### 15.6 Unbounded evolutionary success

More revenue, tasks, users, model score, or past success does not entitle an
Agent or institution to more authority. Power changes require separate review.

## 16. Implementation Directives

After ratification, every major TOS repository and proposal MUST state whether
and how this Constitution applies. Within that declared scope, they SHOULD:

- identify which constitutional articles they implement or depend on;
- distinguish normative specifications from design proposals and evidence
  reports;
- publish normative protocols independently of any one reference
  implementation;
- provide implementation-neutral schemas, examples, conformance suites, and
  compatibility rules;
- state the licenses for specifications, tests, and reference code;
- make conformance testable without importing TOS implementation code;
- document the protocol version, profile, authority domain, extensions, and
  known deviations of each implementation;
- declare the authority owner and stability level of public surfaces;
- preserve exact repository and protocol boundaries;
- define verification, failure, dispute, revocation, and recovery behavior;
- use bounded experiments before expanding irreversible authority;
- publish known gaps and blocked gates; and
- update cross-references when a constitutional amendment changes their
  governing assumptions.

Existing documents do not become compliant merely because they are linked
here. Contradictions should be recorded and resolved through normal review.

Design prose, source presence, generated schemas, local tests, and a reference
implementation are different evidence classes. None of them alone proves
public deployment, independent interoperability, production safety, external
demand, legal compliance, or finalized economic activity.

## 17. Constitutional Compliance and Accountability

### 17.1 Compliance records

Each major TOS repository, normative protocol, production release, and shared
institution within the adopted scope MUST maintain a reviewable compliance
record that identifies:

- the applicable constitutional version and exact scope;
- a status of `CONFORMANT`, `PARTIAL`, `NON_APPLICABLE`, or `EXCEPTION`;
- the claim issuer, assessment method, evidence date, and exact artifact or
  deployment identifiers;
- the responsible owner and independent reviewer where required;
- implemented articles and evidence references;
- known contradictions, missing evidence, and blocked gates;
- approved exceptions, expiry, and remediation owner; and
- the last review event and the next required review trigger.

An `EXCEPTION` MUST be explicit, narrowly scoped, justified by evidence,
time-bounded where possible, and unable to silently amend the Constitution.
It MUST NOT derogate from the Entrenched Core. Related, repeated, or chained
exceptions MUST be assessed cumulatively with amendments and emergencies that
have substantially the same effect.
`PARTIAL` is an honest implementation status, not permission to claim full
constitutional conformance.

### 17.2 Review triggers

Compliance MUST be reviewed when a major protocol or authority boundary
changes, before a production release with new consequential powers, after a
material incident, when a critical dependency or license changes, and at a
documented periodic interval appropriate to the risk.

High-risk surfaces SHOULD receive review by a party independent of the
implementation owner. Review evidence SHOULD be reproducible without access to
private claims or privileged test paths. Confidential material MAY be
protected, but its absence MUST be disclosed and the resulting assurance
limited accordingly.

### 17.3 Violations and remedies

A constitutional violation does not retroactively erase finalized state,
invalidate every dependent Agreement, or authorize an unrecorded rollback. It
requires the responsible authority to preserve evidence, stop unsupported
claims and unsafe new effects, notify affected parties where appropriate, and
publish a remediation, migration, exception, or amendment decision.

A Critical Violation affecting human authority, custody, domain separation,
evidence integrity, open conformance, or emergency constraints MUST block a
claim of constitutional conformance and any new production activation that
depends on the violated authority. A narrowly scoped release whose sole purpose
is containment or remediation MAY proceed through the emergency or exception
process with explicit residual-risk disclosure and independent verification.

Constitutional conformance, protocol conformance, security assessment,
deployment evidence, and legal compliance are separate claims. No TOS body or
third party may combine them into a single self-issued badge without publishing
the exact criteria and supporting evidence.

## Closing Commitment

The Agentic Internet is larger than TOS. TOS will not attempt to know
everything for everyone or require every participant to run TOS code. It will
help build open institutions through which different participants can use
their own knowledge, make exact commitments, prove what matters, resolve
conflict, limit power, and cooperate without surrendering all judgment to one
center.

TOS succeeds as a protocol designer when a third party can read a licensed
specification, build a compatible implementation in an independent repository,
pass the same public conformance tests, and interoperate without asking TOS for
discretionary permission.

An identified consensus domain provides common truth where common truth is
necessary. Protocols provide common language without requiring common code.
Agents provide local intelligence where local intelligence is superior.
Humans remain the source of legitimate purpose and the ultimate stewards of
delegated power.
